The syllabus is **graduation level** for IX–X and **post-graduation level** for XI–XII. It is *not* the school textbook syllabus. XI–XII covers everything in IX–X, but deeper, and adds Real Analysis, Mechanics II, full Probability & Statistics and Numerical Analysis.

## Class IX–X: Mathematics (GRAD)

### I. Algebra
**A. Classical Algebra**
1. **Complex numbers:** definition as ordered pairs of reals; algebra, modulus, amplitude, conjugate, Argand diagram; De Moivre's theorem and applications; exponential, sine, cosine and logarithm of a complex number; definition of aᶻ (a ≠ 0); inverse circular and hyperbolic functions.
2. **Polynomials:**
   - synthetic division and the remainder theorem
   - fundamental theorem of classical algebra (statement only); an nth-degree equation has exactly n roots
   - nature of roots (surd and complex roots occur in pairs); Descartes' rule of signs
   - general properties of equations; multiple roots; Rolle's theorem and its application
   - relations between roots and coefficients; symmetric functions of roots
   - transformation of equations; Cardan's method for the cubic
3. **Determinants (up to order 3):** definition, properties, minors and cofactors; product of two determinants; adjoint, symmetric and skew-symmetric determinants; Cramer's rule (≤ 3 variables).
4. **Matrices of real numbers:**
   - equality, addition, scalar multiplication, multiplication
   - scalar and identity matrices; inverse of a non-singular matrix
   - elementary operations; rank (by minors or by the sweep-out process)
   - consistency and solution of linear systems (≤ 3 variables) by the matrix method

**B. Modern Algebra**
1. **Sets and mappings:** sets, subsets, equality, union, intersection, complement; laws of the algebra of sets, De Morgan's laws; Cartesian product; one-one and onto mappings, composition, identity and inverse mappings.
2. **Groups:** definition; examples from number systems, roots of unity, 2×2 real matrices and non-singular real matrices; elementary properties; subgroups.
3. **Rings and fields:** ring, field, sub-ring, sub-field (definitions and examples).
4. **Vector spaces over a field:** linear combinations; linear dependence and independence; subspace; generators and basis of a finite-dimensional space.
5. Real quadratic forms in ≤ 3 variables (problems only).
6. Characteristic equation of a square matrix (order ≤ 3); eigenvalues and eigenvectors (problems only); the Cayley–Hamilton theorem (statement and illustration).

### II. Geometry
**A. Two dimensions**
1. Transformation of rectangular axes: translation, rotation, invariants.
2. General second-degree equation: reduction to canonical form, classification of conics.
3. Pair of straight lines:
   - condition for the general second-degree equation to be a pair of lines; point of intersection
   - angle between the lines ax² + 2hxy + by² = 0; bisectors
   - lines joining the origin to the points where a line meets a conic
4. Pair of tangents from an external point, chord of contact, poles and polars (ellipse, hyperbola).
5. Polar equations of lines, circles, and of a conic with a focus as pole; chord, tangents and normals.

**B. Three dimensions**
1. Cartesian coordinates, distance, section formula; direction cosines and ratios; projection; angle between lines.
2. Plane: general, intercept and normal forms; angle between planes; signed distance of a point; bisectors.
3. Straight line: general and symmetric forms; distance of a point from a line; coplanarity; shortest distance between skew lines.

### III. Differential Calculus
1. **Real numbers:** rational and irrational numbers; the real line as a linear continuum; basic properties (no proofs).
2. **Sequences:** bounds, monotone sequences, limits; convergence of monotone sequences; definition of e; Cauchy's general principle (statement).
3. **Infinite series:**
   - convergence and divergence; Cauchy's principle (application only)
   - comparison, D'Alembert's ratio and Cauchy's root tests
   - alternating series and the Leibniz test
4. **Functions on an interval:** limit (Cauchy's definition), algebra of limits, continuity; properties of continuous functions on a closed interval (no proof); inverse of a strictly monotone function.
5. **Derivative:**
   - geometric and physical meaning; monotonicity
   - continuity vs derivability; approximation
   - successive derivatives; Leibniz's theorem
6. Rolle's theorem; Lagrange's mean value theorem; indeterminate forms and L'Hospital's rule; maxima and minima (one variable) with applications.
7. **Functions of two variables:**
   - limit and continuity; partial derivatives; chain rule; implicit differentiation
   - Schwarz's theorem; Euler's theorem on homogeneous functions
   - maxima and minima
8. Tangent and normal; envelopes (problems only).

### IV. Integral Calculus
1. ∫dx/(a + b cos x), ∫(l sin x + m cos x)/(n sin x + p cos x) dx; integration of rational functions.
2. Definite integrals; integration as the limit of a sum (equal intervals).
3. Reduction formulae for ∫sinᵐx cosⁿx dx, ∫sinᵐx/sinⁿx dx, ∫tanⁿx dx (m, n non-negative integers).
4. Working knowledge of double integrals.
5. Rectification, quadrature, volumes and surface areas of solids of revolution.

### V. Differential Equations
1. Order, degree, formation of ODEs; first-order equations:
   - variables separable
   - homogeneous and reducible to homogeneous
   - linear and Bernoulli equations
   - Clairaut's equation (general and singular solutions)
2. Second-order linear equations with constant coefficients; Euler's homogeneous equations.

### VI. Vector Algebra
Vectors and scalars; addition and scalar multiplication; collinear and coplanar vectors; scalar and vector products of two and of three vectors; simple applications to geometry.

### VII. Analytical Dynamics
1. Velocity and acceleration in Cartesian and polar coordinates; tangential and normal components.
2. Newton's laws; work, power, energy; conservation of energy and momentum; impulsive forces; rectilinear motion.
3. Rectilinear motion under:
   - constant and variable forces
   - SHM and the inverse square law
   - forced and damped oscillation
   - an elastic string

   Also: the energy equation and conservative forces.
4. Projectiles in vacuo and in a medium with resistance ∝ velocity; motion under forces varying with distance from a fixed point.
5. Central orbits.

### VIII. Linear Programming
- Formulation of an LPP and its matrix form.
- Convex sets, hyperplanes, extreme points, convex polyhedra.
- Basic and basic feasible solutions; the fundamental theorem (statement); reducing a feasible solution to a BFS.
- Standard form; graphical method (two variables); simplex method (≤ 4 variables); feasibility and optimality conditions.
- Penalty (Big-M) method.
- Duality: the dual of the dual is the primal; relation between the objective values.
- Transportation and assignment problems.

### IX. Numerical Methods
1. Approximate numbers, significant figures, rounding; absolute, relative and percentage error.
2. Operators Δ, ∇ and E.
3. Interpolation: difference tables; Newton's forward and backward formulae.
4. Numerical integration: the trapezoidal rule and Simpson's 1/3 rule.
5. Roots of equations: tabulation, bisection, Newton–Raphson.

### X. Probability and Statistics
1. Variables, population and sample; discrete and continuous variables; frequency distributions.
2. Mean, median, mode.
3. Range, mean deviation, standard deviation, variance.
4. Sample space, events, random variables; the classical definition of probability; total, compound and conditional probability; Bayes' theorem.

## Class XI–XII: Mathematics (PG)

### I. Algebra
**A. Classical Algebra**
1. **Integers:**
   - well-ordering principle; first and second principles of induction
   - divisibility; the division algorithm; GCD (existence and uniqueness); coprime integers
   - primes; Euclid's first and second theorems; the unique factorisation theorem
2. **Complex numbers:** as for IX–X, including aᶻ and inverse circular and hyperbolic functions.
3. **Polynomials with real coefficients:**
   - as for IX–X, plus reciprocal equations
   - Cardan's method (cubic)
   - Ferrari's method (biquadratic)
4. **Inequalities:** AM ≥ GM ≥ HM; Cauchy's inequality.

**B. Modern Algebra**
1. **Sets, relations and mappings:**
   - sets: symmetric difference, De Morgan's laws
   - relations: domain and range; equivalence relations and partitions; partial order
   - congruence modulo n and congruence classes
   - mappings: injection, surjection, bijection; composition and associativity
2. **Group theory:**
   - Groupoid, semigroup, monoid, group; ℤₙ under addition and multiplication; Klein's 4-group.
   - Order of a group and of an element; subgroups (necessary and sufficient condition), their intersection and union.
   - Cosets and Lagrange's theorem.
   - Cyclic groups and generators.
   - Permutations, cycles, transpositions, even and odd permutations; the symmetric and alternating groups.
   - Normal subgroups and quotient groups.
   - Homomorphism, isomorphism, kernel; the fundamental theorem. An infinite cyclic group ≅ (ℤ, +); a finite cyclic group of order n ≅ ℤₙ.
3. **Rings and fields:** ring of integers mod n; integral domains; fields; sub-rings and sub-fields; characteristic.

**C. Matrix Theory and Linear Algebra**
1. Real and complex matrices: operations, transpose, symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices.
2. Determinants: properties, minors and cofactors, Cramer's rule (order ≤ 3).
3. **Adjoint, rank and normal forms:**
   - A·adj A = adj A·A = (det A)·I; singular and invertible matrices
   - elementary operations and rank; elementary matrices and normal form
   - equivalence and congruence; diagonalisation
   - real quadratic forms in three variables, reduced to normal form
4. **Vector spaces:**
   - subspaces; sum and intersection; linear span
   - generators and basis; extending a linearly independent set to a basis
   - special emphasis on ℝⁿ
5. Row space and column space; row rank = column rank = rank.
6. Linear systems: the solution space of a homogeneous system; condition for a non-trivial solution; consistency of non-homogeneous systems; the matrix method.
7. **Linear transformations:**
   - null space and range; rank and nullity
   - Sylvester's law of nullity
   - matrix of a transformation with respect to ordered bases
8. **Inner product spaces:**
   - norm; Euclidean space
   - triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities
   - orthogonality and orthonormal bases; Gram–Schmidt orthonormalisation
9. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors; characteristic equation; Cayley–Hamilton; properties of eigenvalues.

### II. Real Analysis
1. Real numbers: Cantor–Dedekind axiom, bounded sets, the least upper bound axiom, the Archimedean property, decimal representation.
2. **Point sets in ℝ and ℝ²:**
   - countability of ℚ; uncountability of ℝ
   - neighbourhoods, interior points, open and closed sets, limit points
   - Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem
   - Lindelöf and Heine–Borel theorems (statements); compact sets
3. **Limits of functions:** bounded and monotone functions; sandwich rule; standard limits (sin x/x, log(1+x)/x, (eˣ−1)/x).
4. **Sequences:**
   - convergence; monotone sequences; the nested interval theorem
   - Cauchy's general principle and Cauchy sequences; e
   - Cauchy's first and second limit theorems; subsequences
5. **Series:**
   - Cauchy's criterion; Abel–Pringsheim, comparison, root and ratio tests
   - absolute and conditional convergence; alternating series and the Leibniz test
   - Abel's and Dirichlet's tests
6. **Continuity:**
   - composite and piecewise continuous functions
   - uniform continuity; types of discontinuity
   - properties on closed intervals; inverse functions
7. **Differentiability:**
   - chain rule; Leibniz theorem
   - Darboux and Rolle theorems; Lagrange and Cauchy mean value theorems
   - Taylor's theorem; Maclaurin series of eˣ, aˣ, log(1+x), (1+x)ᵐ, sin x and cos x with ranges of validity
8. Indeterminate forms and L'Hospital's rule.
9. Maxima and minima: sufficient conditions; applications.
10. **Curves:**
    - tangents and normals; pedal equations
    - asymptotes (Cartesian and parametric)
    - curvature: radius, centre and chord of curvature
    - curve tracing
11. Integrals of sinᵐx cosⁿx, tanⁿx, secⁿx, (l cos x + m sin x)/(p cos x + q sin x), 1/(a + cos x)ⁿ, 1/(x² + a²)ⁿ (reduction formulae).
12. Area enclosed by a curve; length of a curve.
13. Sequences of functions: pointwise and uniform convergence; Cauchy's criterion; continuity and differentiability of the limit.
14. Series of functions: uniform convergence; Abel's and Dirichlet's tests; term-by-term continuity, integration and differentiation.
15. **Power series:**
    - radius of convergence; the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem
    - Abel's limit theorem; uniqueness
    - exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions defined by power series
16. **Riemann integration:**
    - upper and lower sums and integrals; Darboux's theorem
    - integrability conditions; integrability of monotone and continuous functions
    - the fundamental theorem
    - first and second mean value theorems (statements)
17. Improper integrals: comparison and μ-tests; absolute convergence; Beta and Gamma functions.
18. Functions of two variables: limit, continuity, partial derivatives, differentiability; chain rule; Euler's theorem; Schwarz's and Young's theorems.

### III. Differential Equations
1. Geometric and physical meaning; formation by eliminating constants; linear and non-linear ODEs.
2. **First order, first degree:**
   - existence theorem (statement)
   - separable, homogeneous and exact equations; integrating factors
   - linear equations and those reducible to linear
3. First order, higher degree: Clairaut's equation; singular solutions.
4. Applications: geometry, orthogonal trajectories.
5. **Higher-order linear equations with constant coefficients:**
   - complementary function and particular integral; the D operator
   - variation of parameters
   - Euler (Cauchy–Euler) equations

### IV. Analytical Geometry
**A. Two dimensions:** as for IX–X (axes transformation and invariants, second-degree equations, pairs of lines). Also, for the circle, parabola, ellipse and hyperbola: tangent pairs, chord of contact, poles and polars, conjugate points and lines; polar equations.

**B. Three dimensions**
1. Coordinates; octants; projections; direction cosines; section formula.
2. **Plane:**
   - forms; sides of a plane; signed distance
   - plane through the intersection of two planes; angle; bisectors
   - parallelism and perpendicularity
3. **Line:**
   - symmetric and parametric forms; line of intersection of two planes
   - angle; distance from a point; coplanarity
   - shortest distance between skew lines
4. Sphere: general equation; circle; sphere through the intersection of two spheres; radical plane; tangent and normal.
5. General second-degree equation in three variables: reduction and classification of quadrics.

### V. Vector Algebra and Analysis
1. **Vector algebra:**
   - free vectors; position vectors; collinearity and coplanarity
   - components; scalar, vector and triple products; products of four vectors
   - applications: geometry, work done, moment of a force, lines and planes, volume of a tetrahedron, shortest distance
2. **Vector analysis:** vector functions of a scalar variable; derivatives of sums and products; velocity and acceleration.

### VI. Mechanics I
1. Coplanar concurrent forces: composition and resolution; moments and couples.
2. Reduction of coplanar force systems; equilibrium conditions.
3. Laws of motion; impulse; work, power, energy; conservation laws.
4. **Rectilinear motion:**
   - variable acceleration; the inverse square law
   - composition of two SHMs; elastic strings
   - resisting media; damped and forced oscillation
   - resistance ∝ vⁿ; terminal velocity
5. Impact of elastic bodies: Newton's experimental law; loss of KE.
6. Velocity and acceleration in Cartesian and polar coordinates; motion in a plane.
7. Central forces and central orbits.
8. Tangential and normal acceleration; circular motion.
9. Motion under resistance; projectiles in a resisting medium.
10. Friction: cone of friction; equilibrium on rough curves and surfaces.
11. Centre of gravity.

### VII. Linear Programming
1. Formulation; graphical solution.
2. **Theory of feasible solutions:**
   - BFS (degenerate and non-degenerate)
   - hyperplanes, convex sets, cones, extreme points, convex hulls and polyhedra
   - supporting and separating hyperplanes; the feasible set is convex
   - extreme points ↔ BFS; the fundamental theorem
3. Slack and surplus variables; standard form; theory of the simplex method.
4. The simplex algorithm; the two-phase method; degeneracy.
5. Duality: complementary slackness; dual simplex.
6. Transportation and assignment problems.

### VIII. Mechanics II
1. Friction (as in VI.10); centre of gravity.
2. Astatic equilibrium; equilibrium on smooth curves.
3. Virtual work: for a particle and for a rigid body; the converse.
4. **Forces in three dimensions:**
   - moment about a line; couples
   - Poinsot's central axis; wrench, pitch, screw
   - invariants
5. **Inverse square law:**
   - escape velocity
   - Kepler's laws; artificial satellites; disturbed orbits
   - conservative fields; motion on rough curves
6. **Rigid dynamics:**
   - Moments and products of inertia: parallel and perpendicular axes; principal axes; momental ellipsoid; equimomental systems.
   - D'Alembert's principle; conservation of linear and angular momentum.
   - Motion about a fixed axis: compound pendulum.
   - Motion in two dimensions: rolling on rough planes; impulsive action.
   - Generalised coordinates; the Lagrangian; cyclic coordinates; the Routhian.

### IX. Probability and Statistics
**A. Probability**
- **Foundations:**
  - classical, frequency and axiomatic definitions
  - Bayes' theorem; independence
  - Bernoulli trials; the multinomial law
- **Random variables and distributions:**
  - binomial, Poisson, uniform, normal, Cauchy, gamma, beta (1st and 2nd kind)
  - the Poisson process
  - transformations; bivariate distributions (including the bivariate normal); conditional distributions
- **Expectation and moments:**
  - mean, variance, moments; skewness and kurtosis
  - MGF and characteristic function
  - covariance and correlation
  - conditional expectation; regression
  - χ² distribution
- **Limit theorems:**
  - Chebyshev's inequality; convergence in probability
  - Bernoulli's theorem; the weak law of large numbers
  - Poisson and normal approximations to the binomial
  - the central limit theorem (equal components)

**B. Statistics**
- **Samples and estimators:**
  - sampling distributions
  - unbiased and consistent estimators
  - exact distributions for normal populations
- **Correlation and regression:** bivariate data; scatter diagrams; correlation; least-squares regression lines and parabolas.
- **Estimation:** maximum likelihood; confidence intervals (normal and binomial).
- **Hypothesis testing:**
  - simple and composite hypotheses; best critical region
  - the Neyman–Pearson lemma
  - likelihood ratio tests

### X. Numerical Analysis
1. Errors: round-off, significant digits, propagation.
2. Interpolation:
   - difference tables
   - Newton forward and backward, Stirling and Bessel formulae
   - divided differences; Lagrange's formula; inverse interpolation
3. Numerical differentiation (Newton, Lagrange); error.
4. Numerical integration: Newton–Cotes; trapezoidal, Simpson's 1/3 and Weddle's rules; error formulae.
5. Roots of equations: tabulation and graphs; bisection; fixed-point iteration (convergence); Newton–Raphson; regula falsi; error estimates.
6. ODEs (first order, first degree): Euler's method, RK4, Milne's method.
