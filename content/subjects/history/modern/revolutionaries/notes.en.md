# Revolutionary Nationalism

## Origins and early groups in Maharashtra {#origins}

Revolutionary nationalism was a strand of the freedom struggle that aimed to end British rule through secret organisation, physical training, arms and individual acts of violence against officials. It developed in **two broad phases**: the first from the late 1890s to the end of the First World War, and the second in the 1920s and early 1930s.

### Why it arose
- Disappointment with the **Moderates' methods** of petitions and appeals, which had produced few results.
- The **Partition of Bengal (1905)** and the harsh repression of the Swadeshi movement convinced many young people that peaceful protest alone would not work.
- After the **Surat split (1907)** and the arrest or exile of Extremist leaders, many young nationalists were left without an organisation to channel their energy.
- Inspiration from the history of **Irish nationalists, Russian revolutionaries** and the Italian unification movement, and from Japan's victory over Russia (1905), which showed that a European power could be defeated by an Asian one.

The early revolutionaries usually worked in **small secret groups** built around physical culture, the study of national history and religious texts, and the collection of arms. They did not try to organise the masses.

### The Chapekar brothers (1897)
In **Pune**, harsh measures taken by the government during the **plague epidemic** of 1896–97 (house searches, forced removal of patients) caused great anger. On **22 June 1897** the brothers **Damodar Hari and Balkrishna Chapekar** shot **W.C. Rand**, chairman of the Special Plague Committee, and **Lt. Ayerst**, an officer travelling with him. Ayerst died on the spot and Rand died of his wounds on 3 July 1897. This is usually described as the **first political assassination** of the revolutionary movement in India. Both brothers were tried and hanged.

### Mitra Mela and Abhinav Bharat
- The **Savarkar brothers**, **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar** and **Ganesh Savarkar**, organised the **Mitra Mela** at **Nasik** in **1899**.
- It was turned into the secret society **Abhinav Bharat** in **1904**, modelled partly on Mazzini's "Young Italy".
- In **1909** the Collector of Nasik, **A.M.T. Jackson**, was shot dead by **Anant Laxman Kanhere**, a member of the group. In the **Nasik Conspiracy Case** that followed, V.D. Savarkar was sentenced to transportation for life and sent to the **Cellular Jail** in the Andamans.

:::keyfacts
- First political assassination: **Rand and Ayerst**, Pune, **1897**, by the **Chapekar brothers**
- **Abhinav Bharat** (1904) grew out of the **Mitra Mela** (1899) of the **Savarkar brothers**, Nasik
- Nasik Collector **Jackson** killed **1909** by **Anant Kanhere**
:::

:::caution
Rand was killed during the **plague** measures in Pune, not during the Partition of Bengal. Do not mix up the two Jacksons: **A.M.T. Jackson** (Nasik Collector, killed 1909) and **Sir Stanley Jackson** (Governor of Bengal, fired at by Bina Das in 1932).
:::

## Anushilan Samiti, Jugantar and the Alipore case {#bengal-societies}

Bengal became the most important centre of revolutionary activity in the first phase. The anger caused by the partition of the province in 1905 gave the secret societies a steady stream of young recruits.

### Anushilan Samiti
- Founded in **Calcutta** in **1902** as a society for physical culture and moral training. Its leading figure was the barrister **Pramathanath Mitra**; others connected with it included Jatindranath Banerjee and Barindra Kumar Ghosh.
- A powerful **Dhaka branch** (Dhaka Anushilan Samiti) was organised in **1906** by **Pulin Behari Das**. It soon became larger than the Calcutta body and spread across eastern Bengal.

### Jugantar
- **Jugantar** began as a **Bengali weekly newspaper** in **1906**, started by **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** (younger brother of Aurobindo Ghosh) and **Bhupendranath Dutta** (younger brother of Swami Vivekananda).
- The paper openly preached revolution, and the name **Jugantar** came to be used for the revolutionary group around Barindra.

### The Muzaffarpur incident (1908)
- The target was **Douglas Kingsford**, who as Chief Presidency Magistrate of Calcutta had passed harsh sentences on young Swadeshi workers and who had then been posted as District Judge at **Muzaffarpur** (Bihar).
- On **30 April 1908**, **Khudiram Bose** and **Prafulla Chaki** threw a bomb at a carriage they believed was carrying Kingsford. It was in fact carrying **two English women** (Mrs and Miss Kennedy), who were killed; Kingsford was unhurt.
- **Prafulla Chaki** shot himself when the police were about to arrest him. **Khudiram Bose**, then about 18 years old, was tried and **hanged on 11 August 1908**.

### The Alipore (Maniktala) Bomb Case (1908–09)
- The police raided the group's centre at a garden house in **Maniktala (Muraripukur)**, Calcutta, and arrested Barindra Kumar Ghosh, **Aurobindo Ghosh** and many others.
- Aurobindo was defended by **Chittaranjan Das** and was **acquitted** in 1909. He soon left politics and settled at **Pondicherry**.
- Barindra Kumar Ghosh and some others were sentenced to death, later reduced to transportation for life in the Andamans.
- **Narendranath Goswami**, an accused who turned approver, was shot dead inside Alipore jail by **Kanailal Dutta** and **Satyendranath Bose**, who were later hanged.

:::keyfacts
- Anushilan Samiti: **1902**, Calcutta, **Pramathanath Mitra**; Dhaka branch **1906**, **Pulin Behari Das**
- *Jugantar* weekly: **1906**, **Barindra Kumar Ghosh** and **Bhupendranath Dutta**
- Muzaffarpur bomb: **30 April 1908**, **Khudiram Bose** and **Prafulla Chaki**; target **Kingsford**
- Alipore Bomb Case: **Aurobindo acquitted** (defence counsel **C.R. Das**)
:::

:::caution
The Muzaffarpur bomb did **not** kill Kingsford; it killed two English women. Aurobindo was **acquitted** in the Alipore case, while his brother **Barindra** was convicted.
:::

## Delhi conspiracy and Bagha Jatin {#delhi-balasore}

The revolutionary movement spread from Bengal to north India, and during the First World War its leaders tried to use Britain's difficulties to organise an armed rising with foreign help.

### Delhi conspiracy (1912)
- On **23 December 1912**, during the ceremonial entry of the Viceroy **Lord Hardinge** into **Delhi** (the new capital), a bomb was thrown at his elephant procession in Chandni Chowk. Hardinge was **wounded but survived**.
- The plan is credited mainly to **Rash Behari Bose**, a Bengali revolutionary working in north India. Several others were tried and executed in the **Delhi Conspiracy Case** (also called the Delhi-Lahore Conspiracy Case).
- Rash Behari Bose escaped arrest. He was also involved in the Ghadar plan for a rising in Punjab in 1915, after which he went to **Japan**. Much later, in **1943**, he handed over the leadership of the Indian Independence League and the Indian National Army in South-East Asia to **Subhas Chandra Bose**.

### Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee)
- **Jatindranath Mukherjee**, popularly called **Bagha Jatin**, became the most important leader of the Jugantar group after the Alipore arrests.
- He was among the accused in the **Howrah–Sibpur Conspiracy Case (1910)** but was released for lack of evidence.
- During the **First World War**, Jugantar leaders tried to obtain **German arms and money** to start an armed uprising in India (often called the **Indo-German plan**). The plan was discovered by the British.
- In **September 1915**, Bagha Jatin and a few companions fought the police near **Balasore** (Odisha), on the bank of the Burhi Balang river. He was badly wounded and died soon afterwards.

:::keyfacts
- Bomb on **Lord Hardinge**: **23 December 1912**, Delhi; planner **Rash Behari Bose**
- **Bagha Jatin** = **Jatindranath Mukherjee**, leader of **Jugantar**; died after the fight near **Balasore**, **1915**
- First World War: revolutionaries sought **German** help for an armed rising
:::

:::caution
Rash Behari Bose (revolutionary, later in Japan) and Subhas Chandra Bose (Congress president, later INA leader) are different people. Rash Behari handed over the INA movement in South-East Asia to Subhas in 1943.
:::

## Revolutionaries abroad and the Ghadar Party {#abroad}

Indian students and emigrants abroad set up centres that published nationalist literature, trained members and sent arms and ideas to India.

### India House, London
- **Shyamji Krishna Varma** founded the **Indian Home Rule Society** and opened **India House** in London in **1905**, as a hostel and meeting place for Indian students. He also brought out the monthly journal **The Indian Sociologist**.
- **V.D. Savarkar** became active at India House after he went to London.
- On **1 July 1909**, **Madan Lal Dhingra**, a student linked to the India House circle, shot dead **Sir William Curzon Wyllie**, an official of the India Office, in London. Dhingra was hanged in August 1909.

### Madame Cama and the Continent
- **Madame Bhikaji Cama**, working from Paris, unfurled an Indian national flag at the **International Socialist Congress at Stuttgart** (Germany) in **1907**.
- During the First World War, Indian revolutionaries formed the **Berlin Committee**, with **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya** as a leading figure, to seek German help.
- A **Provisional Government of India** was set up at **Kabul** in **1915**, with **Raja Mahendra Pratap** as President and **Maulana Barkatullah** as Prime Minister.

### The Ghadar Party (1913)
- Formed in **1913**, with its headquarters at **San Francisco** (USA), by Indian immigrants on the Pacific coast of North America, most of them **Punjabi** (largely Sikh) farmers and workers. (The founding meeting is often placed at Astoria, Oregon; exam books usually name San Francisco.)
- **Lala Har Dayal** was its main ideologue; **Sohan Singh Bhakna** was its first president. Its headquarters was called **Yugantar Ashram**.
- It published a weekly paper named **Ghadar** ("revolt") in several languages and called on Indians to return home and drive out the British.
- During the First World War thousands of Ghadarites returned to India. A rising planned for **February 1915** in Punjab was betrayed. Many were tried in the **Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915)**; among those executed was the young **Kartar Singh Sarabha**.

### The Komagata Maru (1914)
- The **Komagata Maru** was a Japanese ship chartered by **Gurdit Singh** to carry Indian emigrants, mostly Punjabis, from Asia to **Vancouver (Canada)**.
- Canadian authorities **refused to let most passengers land** under rules that kept out Indian immigrants. The ship was forced to return.
- At **Budge Budge** near Calcutta in **September 1914**, a clash with the police led to firing in which a number of passengers were killed. The incident deeply angered Indians abroad and helped the Ghadar cause.

:::keyfacts
- **India House**, London: **1905**, **Shyamji Krishna Varma**; journal **The Indian Sociologist**
- **Madan Lal Dhingra** killed **Curzon Wyllie**: London, **1909**
- **Ghadar Party**: **1913**, **San Francisco**; **Lala Har Dayal**, **Sohan Singh Bhakna**
- **Komagata Maru**: **1914**, refused entry at **Vancouver**; firing at **Budge Budge**
:::

:::exam-tip
"Yugantar Ashram" (Ghadar headquarters, San Francisco) and the **Jugantar** group of Bengal share a name but are different bodies. Read the place in the question carefully.
:::

## The HRA and the Kakori case {#hra-kakori}

The second phase began after the **withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922** following the Chauri Chaura incident. Many young people who had joined that movement felt let down and turned to revolutionary methods. Unlike the first phase, the new groups were influenced by **socialist ideas** and the **Russian Revolution (1917)**, and they spoke of a republic and of the rights of workers and peasants.

### Hindustan Republican Association (1924)
- Founded in **October 1924** at **Kanpur**, with **Sachindranath Sanyal**, **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee** and **Ram Prasad Bismil** among its organisers.
- Aim: to set up a **Federal Republic of the United States of India** through an organised armed revolution.
- Its strongest base was in the **United Provinces** (present Uttar Pradesh).

### The Kakori train action (1925)
- To raise funds, HRA members stopped a train at **Kakori**, near **Lucknow**, on **9 August 1925**, and took away the government cash being carried on it.
- The police arrested most of the group. In the **Kakori Conspiracy Case**, four were sentenced to death and hanged in **1927**: **Ram Prasad Bismil**, **Ashfaqulla Khan**, **Roshan Singh** and **Rajendra Lahiri**. Several others received long prison sentences.
- **Chandrashekhar Azad** escaped arrest and later reorganised the group.

The case is remembered for the friendship between **Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan**, often cited as an example of Hindu–Muslim unity in the movement.

:::keyfacts
- HRA: **1924**, **Kanpur**; Sachindranath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee, Ram Prasad Bismil
- Kakori train action: **9 August 1925**, near Lucknow
- Hanged (1927): **Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Roshan Singh, Rajendra Lahiri**
- **Chandrashekhar Azad** escaped
:::

:::caution
Chandrashekhar Azad was **not** executed in the Kakori case. He died in an encounter with the police at **Alfred Park, Allahabad**, on **27 February 1931**.
:::

## The HSRA and Bhagat Singh {#hsra}

### Formation (1928)
- In **September 1928**, at a meeting at **Feroz Shah Kotla** in Delhi, the HRA was reorganised as the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)** (also called the Hindustan Socialist Republican Army).
- Leaders included **Chandrashekhar Azad**, **Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev**, **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** and **Bijoy Kumar Sinha**.
- The addition of the word **"Socialist"** showed that the group now accepted socialism as its goal. Earlier, in **1926**, Bhagat Singh had helped form the **Naujawan Bharat Sabha** in Lahore as an open youth organisation.

### Saunders killing (1928)
- In **October 1928**, during a demonstration against the **Simon Commission** at **Lahore**, **Lala Lajpat Rai** was injured in a police lathi charge. He died in **November 1928**.
- To avenge his death, HSRA members planned to kill the police officer **James Scott**, whom they held responsible. On **17 December 1928**, by mistake, they shot dead **J.P. Saunders**, an Assistant Superintendent of Police, at Lahore. **Bhagat Singh**, **Rajguru** and **Chandrashekhar Azad** were involved.

### Central Legislative Assembly bomb (1929)
- On **8 April 1929**, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** threw two bombs from the visitors' gallery into the **Central Legislative Assembly** in Delhi.
- The bombs were deliberately made **not to kill**; nobody died. The protest was against the **Public Safety Bill** and the **Trade Disputes Bill**.
- They threw leaflets, raised the slogan **"Inquilab Zindabad"** and **did not try to escape**, choosing arrest so that they could use the trial to spread their ideas.

### Trials and executions
- In jail, the revolutionaries went on hunger strike for better treatment of political prisoners. **Jatin Das** died on **13 September 1929** after a **63-day** fast in Lahore jail.
- In the **Lahore Conspiracy Case** (the Saunders case), **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru** were sentenced to death and **hanged on 23 March 1931** in Lahore jail. Batukeshwar Dutt was sentenced to transportation for life in the Assembly case.
- **Bhagwati Charan Vohra** wrote **"The Philosophy of the Bomb"** (1930), a reply to Gandhi's article "The Cult of the Bomb". In prison, Bhagat Singh wrote the essay **"Why I am an Atheist"**.

:::keyfacts
- HSRA: **1928**, **Feroz Shah Kotla**, Delhi; "Socialist" added
- Saunders killed: **17 December 1928**, Lahore (to avenge **Lala Lajpat Rai**)
- Assembly bomb: **8 April 1929**, **Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt**
- Executed **23 March 1931**: **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru**
:::

:::mnemonic
**S**aunders, **A**ssembly, **J**atin Das, **H**anging: "**S**ome **A**pples **J**ust **H**ang" (Dec 1928, Apr 1929, Sep 1929, Mar 1931).
:::

## Bengal in the 1930s: Chittagong and after {#bengal-1930s}

In Bengal the older Anushilan and Jugantar networks revived, and new groups appeared. A notable feature of this phase was the **active participation of women**.

### Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930)
- **Surya Sen** (popularly **Masterda**), a schoolteacher, led a group that called itself the **Indian Republican Army, Chittagong Branch**.
- On **18 April 1930** the group seized the police and auxiliary force armouries at **Chittagong**, cut communications and declared a provisional revolutionary government. Ganesh Ghosh, Lokenath Bal, Ambika Chakraborty and Ananta Singh were among the participants.
- On **22 April 1930** the revolutionaries fought British troops on the **Jalalabad hills**; several were killed. The survivors spread out and carried on guerrilla action.
- Surya Sen was arrested in **February 1933** and **hanged in January 1934**.

### Women revolutionaries
- **Pritilata Waddedar** led an attack on the **Pahartali European Club** (Chittagong) in **September 1932**; she took poison to avoid capture.
- **Kalpana Dutta** was a member of Surya Sen's group; she was arrested and sentenced to transportation for life.
- **Santi Ghosh** and **Suniti Chaudhuri**, schoolgirls, shot dead the District Magistrate of **Comilla** in **December 1931**.
- **Bina Das** fired at the Governor of Bengal, **Sir Stanley Jackson**, at the **Calcutta University convocation** in **February 1932**. He escaped unhurt.

### The Writers' Building attack (1930)
- On **8 December 1930**, **Benoy Basu**, **Badal Gupta** and **Dinesh Gupta**, members of the **Bengal Volunteers**, entered the **Writers' Building** (the Bengal government secretariat in Calcutta) and shot dead **Lt. Col. N.S. Simpson**, Inspector-General of Prisons, who was known for ill-treating prisoners.
- A gun battle followed in the corridors, remembered in Bengali as the *alinda yuddha* (corridor battle). Badal took poison, Benoy shot himself and died in hospital, and Dinesh was captured and **hanged in 1931**.
- Dalhousie Square in Calcutta was later renamed **B.B.D. Bagh** in memory of the three.

:::keyfacts
- **Chittagong Armoury Raid**: **18 April 1930**, **Surya Sen**, Indian Republican Army; Jalalabad fight 22 April 1930
- **Writers' Building**: **8 December 1930**, **Benoy–Badal–Dinesh**; Simpson killed
- **Pritilata Waddedar**: Pahartali European Club, 1932
- **Bina Das**: fired at Governor Stanley Jackson, 1932
:::

:::exam-tip
WBCS often asks about Bengal names: who belonged to which group and which target. Link **Surya Sen, Pritilata, Kalpana Dutta** to **Chittagong**, and **Benoy–Badal–Dinesh** to the **Writers' Building** and **B.B.D. Bagh**.
:::

## Assessment and quick revision {#assessment}

### Government response
- New laws such as the **Explosive Substances Act** and the **Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act** (both **1908**) and the **Defence of India Act (1915)** were used against revolutionaries, along with conspiracy trials, detention without trial and transportation to the **Cellular Jail** in the Andamans.
- The **Sedition Committee** (1918), headed by **Justice S.A.T. Rowlatt**, reported on revolutionary activity; its recommendations led to the **Rowlatt Act (1919)**.

### Contribution
- Their trials and the sacrifices involved made names like **Khudiram, Bhagat Singh and Surya Sen** widely known, raising national feeling, especially among the young.
- The second phase moved from individual heroism towards a **wider political programme**: the HSRA spoke of socialism, of a secular republic and of the rights of workers and peasants. At his trial Bhagat Singh explained that, for him, revolution meant changing the social order, not merely using bombs and pistols.
- They brought **women** into direct political action in Bengal.

### Limitations
- They worked in **small secret groups** and did not build a **mass base**; most did not try to mobilise peasants and workers directly.
- Individual killings could not overthrow a well-organised state, and they invited severe repression. Many leaders were killed, hanged or imprisoned.
- Some first-phase groups drew on religious symbolism, which limited their appeal to all communities.
- Many survivors later joined the **Congress**, the **communist movement** or other mass organisations.
