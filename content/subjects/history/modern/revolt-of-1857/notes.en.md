# The Revolt of 1857

## Background {#background}

By the mid-19th century the East India Company controlled most of India, directly or through subsidiary alliances. Years of annexation, economic exploitation and social interference had built up deep resentment among many groups: rulers, landlords, peasants, artisans and especially the sepoys of the Bengal Army. In 1857 this resentment broke out as the most serious challenge to British rule up to that time.

:::keyfacts
- Governor-General during the revolt: **Lord Canning**
- Began: **10 May 1857** at **Meerut**
- Symbolic leader: **Bahadur Shah II (Zafar)**, the last Mughal emperor
:::

## Causes {#causes}

### Political
- **Doctrine of Lapse** (Lord Dalhousie): a state whose ruler died without a natural heir was annexed; adopted heirs were not recognised. Annexed: **Satara (1848)**, **Jhansi (1853)**, **Nagpur (1854)**, among others.
- **Annexation of Awadh (1856)** on grounds of alleged *misgovernance*. This deeply affected the sepoys, many of whom came from Awadh.
- Pension of **Nana Saheb**, adopted son of the last Peshwa Baji Rao II, was stopped.
- The British announced that Bahadur Shah's successors would lose the imperial title and would have to leave the Red Fort.

:::caution
Awadh was **not** annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse. It was annexed in 1856 on grounds of misgovernance.
:::

### Economic
- Heavy land revenue demands; dispossession of peasants and taluqdars (especially in Awadh).
- Ruin of traditional handicrafts due to cheap British manufactured goods.
- Drain of wealth from India to Britain.

### Socio-religious
- Fear of forced conversion, fuelled by missionary activity.
- Laws seen as interference: the **Religious Disabilities Act, 1850** (allowed converts to inherit ancestral property) and the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**.

### Military
- Low pay and poor prospects of promotion for Indian sepoys.
- **General Service Enlistment Act, 1856** (Canning): new recruits had to serve anywhere, including overseas. Crossing the sea was a religious taboo for many.
- Withdrawal of the foreign service allowance (*bhatta*).

### Immediate cause
The new **Enfield rifle** used cartridges that were rumoured to be greased with **cow and pig fat** and had to be bitten open. This offended both Hindu and Muslim sepoys.

## Outbreak and spread {#outbreak}

:::timeline
- Feb 1857 | 19th Native Infantry at Berhampore refuses to use the new cartridges
- 29 Mar 1857 | **Mangal Pandey** of the **34th Native Infantry** attacks officers at **Barrackpore**; he is hanged on 8 April
- 10 May 1857 | Sepoys at **Meerut** revolt, free imprisoned comrades and kill officers
- 11 May 1857 | Rebels reach **Delhi**; Bahadur Shah II is proclaimed Emperor of Hindustan
- Jun–Jul 1857 | Revolt spreads to Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, Bareilly, Bihar and other areas
:::

The revolt spread mainly across the **Gangetic plain and central India**. **Punjab, Bengal and most of South India remained largely quiet.** Civilian participation, by peasants, artisans and taluqdars, was significant in Awadh and parts of western Bihar.

## Suppression {#suppression}

- **Delhi** was recaptured in **September 1857**. John Nicholson died in the assault. Bahadur Shah II was captured by Hodson, tried, and **exiled to Rangoon**, where he died in 1862.
- **Lucknow** was relieved and finally recaptured by **Colin Campbell** in March 1858.
- **Jhansi** fell to **Hugh Rose** in April 1858. Rani Lakshmibai died fighting near **Gwalior** in June 1858.
- **Tantia Tope** continued guerrilla warfare, was betrayed, captured and executed in **April 1859**.
- Nana Saheb escaped to Nepal.

### Why the revolt failed
- No unified leadership, central command or common plan.
- Limited geographical spread.
- Most princes and big zamindars (e.g. the **Scindia of Gwalior**, the **Nizam of Hyderabad**, the **Holkar**) stayed loyal to the British. The Scindia's troops, however, joined the rebels.
- The modern educated Indian class did not support it.
- The British had superior arms, resources, the telegraph and control of the seas.

## Nature of the revolt {#nature}

Historians differ:

| View | Held by |
|---|---|
| A "Sepoy Mutiny" | Many contemporary British officials and writers |
| **"The Indian War of Independence"** (book, 1909) | **V.D. Savarkar** |
| "Neither the First, nor National, nor a War of Independence" | **R.C. Majumdar** |
| A popular revolt with strong civilian participation, especially in Awadh | Many modern historians (e.g. S.B. Chaudhuri on "civil rebellions") |

:::exam-tip
UPSC asks about the *nature* of the revolt and historians' views. Remember the Savarkar and Majumdar positions, and that **Hindu–Muslim unity** was a notable feature of the revolt.
:::

## Consequences {#consequences}

- **Government of India Act, 1858**:
  - Ended the rule of the East India Company. Power was transferred to the **British Crown**.
  - Created the post of **Secretary of State for India**, assisted by a **Council of India (15 members)**.
  - Abolished the **Board of Control** and the **Court of Directors**.
  - The Governor-General also became the **Viceroy**. **Lord Canning** became the first Viceroy.
- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation** was read by Canning at **Allahabad on 1 November 1858**:
  - The **Doctrine of Lapse** was abandoned; the princes' right to adopt heirs was recognised.
  - Promised non-interference in religious matters and equal treatment of Indians.
- **Army reorganisation** (Peel Commission): a higher proportion of European soldiers; artillery kept in European hands; recruitment favoured groups considered "loyal".
- The British increasingly followed a **divide and rule** policy.
- The **Mughal dynasty** came to an end.
