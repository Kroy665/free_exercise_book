# Socio-Religious Reform Movements

## Background {#background}

In the 19th century a new educated class in India began to question social practices such as sati, child marriage, the ban on widow remarriage, caste discrimination and the denial of education to women. Reformers appealed both to **reason** and to their own religious texts. The movement began in **Bengal**, and this phase of intellectual and cultural awakening is often called the **Bengal Renaissance**.

### Factors behind the reform movements
- **Western education and ideas**: rationalism, humanism and liberal thought reached Indians through English education. Lord Macaulay's Minute (1835) and the English Education Act (1835) under **Lord William Bentinck** made English the medium of higher education. **Wood's Despatch (1854)** led to the founding of universities at **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras in 1857**.
- **Printing press and newspapers**: cheap printed books and journals spread new ideas in Indian languages. Hicky's *Bengal Gazette* (1780) was the first newspaper in India. Reformers ran their own papers to argue their case.
- **Christian missionaries**: their criticism of Hindu customs pushed Indian thinkers to reform and defend their own traditions. The Serampore missionaries were also early printers in Bengali.
- **Oriental scholarship**: the work of scholars of the Asiatic Society (founded by William Jones in 1784) made Indians aware of their ancient texts and past.

### Two broad trends
| Trend | Approach | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Reformist | Change society from within, using reason and selected scriptures | Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement |
| Revivalist | Return to what they saw as a purer form of the old religion | Arya Samaj, Deoband Movement |

:::caution
"Reformist" and "revivalist" are broad labels used by textbooks. Most movements combined both elements; for example, the Arya Samaj opposed caste rigidity and child marriage while calling for a return to the Vedas.
:::

:::keyfacts
- Starting point of the reform movement: **Bengal**
- Common targets: **sati, child marriage, polygamy, ban on widow remarriage, caste rigidity, idol worship and ritualism**
- Main tools: **press, schools, voluntary associations and petitions for new laws**
:::

## Raja Rammohun Roy {#rammohun-roy}

**Raja Rammohun Roy (1772–1833)** is widely called the father of the Indian Renaissance and the pioneer of modern India. He knew Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic and English, and studied several religions. He believed in **one formless God (monotheism)** and opposed idol worship and rituals he considered meaningless. He argued that the **Upanishads** supported his views.

### Main activities
- Wrote **Tuhfat-ul-Muwahhidin** (in Persian), a critique of idolatry and superstition.
- Founded the **Atmiya Sabha** in **Calcutta in 1814** (some sources give 1815) to spread monotheistic ideas.
- Founded the **Brahmo Sabha** on **20 August 1828**; it was later renamed the **Brahmo Samaj**.
- Supported **David Hare's** efforts to found the **Hindu College (1817)** and set up the **Vedanta College (1825)**, which combined Indian learning with Western science.
- Ran newspapers such as **Sambad Kaumudi** (Bengali) and **Mirat-ul-Akhbar** (Persian).
- The Mughal emperor **Akbar II** gave him the title of **"Raja"** and sent him to England to present the emperor's grievances. He died at **Bristol, England, in 1833**.

### Campaign against sati
Rammohun Roy showed that the old scriptures did not require a widow to burn herself on her husband's pyre. He wrote and petitioned against the practice. Governor-General **Lord William Bentinck** declared sati illegal and punishable by **Regulation XVII of 1829** (December 1829), first in the **Bengal Presidency**; it was later extended to the other presidencies.

### Other views
- Supported **women's right to inherit property**, and opposed polygamy.
- Favoured **modern Western education**, especially science, along with Indian learning.
- Supported freedom of the press and raised political questions such as better treatment of Indians in administration.

:::keyfacts
- Atmiya Sabha: **1814** (some sources: 1815)
- Brahmo Sabha: **20 August 1828**
- Sati banned: **Regulation XVII, 1829**, Lord **William Bentinck**
- Title "Raja" given by **Akbar II**; died at **Bristol (1833)**
:::

:::caution
The ban on sati came through a **regulation of 1829**, not an Act of 1856. The **1856 Act** concerns **widow remarriage** and is linked with **Vidyasagar**.
:::

## The Brahmo Samaj after Rammohun {#brahmo-samaj}

After Rammohun's death the Brahmo Samaj weakened, and was revived by **Debendranath Tagore** (father of Rabindranath Tagore).

### Debendranath Tagore
- Founded the **Tattvabodhini Sabha (1839)** and its journal, the **Tattvabodhini Patrika**, to spread Rammohun's ideas in Bengali.
- Joined the Brahmo Samaj in the early 1840s and gave it new life and organisation.

### Keshab Chandra Sen and the first split (1866)
- **Keshab Chandra Sen** joined the Samaj in the late 1850s and was made its *acharya* (preacher) by Debendranath.
- Keshab was energetic and radical. He took the Samaj's message outside Bengal and campaigned for **inter-caste marriage, widow remarriage and women's education**, and against the caste system.
- Disagreements over the pace of social reform led to a split in **1866**:
  - Debendranath's group became the **Adi Brahmo Samaj**.
  - Keshab's group became the **Brahmo Samaj of India**.
- Keshab's campaign led to the **Native Marriage Act, 1872**, popularly called the **Brahmo Marriage Act**. It legalised Brahmo marriages and inter-caste marriages, and fixed a minimum age of marriage (14 for girls, 18 for boys).

### The second split (1878)
- In **1878** Keshab married his under-age daughter to the young **Maharaja of Cooch Behar**, against the very rule of the 1872 Act he had campaigned for.
- Many of his followers left and formed the **Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (1878)**. Its leaders included **Ananda Mohan Bose** and **Sivanath Sastri**.
- Keshab then founded the **Naba Bidhan** (New Dispensation).

:::timeline
- 1828 | Brahmo Sabha founded by Rammohun Roy
- 1839 | Tattvabodhini Sabha founded by Debendranath Tagore
- 1866 | First split: Adi Brahmo Samaj and Brahmo Samaj of India
- 1872 | Native (Brahmo) Marriage Act
- 1878 | Second split: Sadharan Brahmo Samaj formed
:::

:::exam-tip
Remember which leader went with which body: **Debendranath – Adi Brahmo Samaj**, **Keshab – Brahmo Samaj of India**, **Ananda Mohan Bose and Sivanath Sastri – Sadharan Brahmo Samaj**.
:::

## Young Bengal and Vidyasagar {#young-bengal-vidyasagar}

### Derozio and the Young Bengal Movement
- **Henry Louis Vivian Derozio (1809–1831)**, an Anglo-Indian, taught at the **Hindu College, Calcutta**, from **1826 to 1831**.
- He encouraged his students to **think freely and question everything**, including religious customs and social practices. He was inspired by the French Revolution and supported women's rights and freedom of the press.
- His radical students were known as **Young Bengal** (the Derozians). They openly broke many social customs.
- Derozio was **removed from the Hindu College in 1831** because of his radical views, and died of cholera the same year.
- The Young Bengal Movement had little lasting mass impact, but it spread rational and critical thinking among the educated class.

### Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–1891)
- A Sanskrit scholar who became **principal of the Sanskrit College, Calcutta**. He opened the college to students who were not Brahmins.
- **Widow remarriage**: using the ancient texts, he argued that widows could remarry. His campaign, supported by many petitions, led to the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**.
- **Girls' education**: as a government inspector of schools he helped open many girls' schools, and he was associated with the **Bethune School** in Calcutta, an early school for girls.
- Campaigned against **child marriage and polygamy**.
- Wrote Bengali primers such as **Barnaparichay**, which shaped Bengali learning for generations.

:::keyfacts
- Derozio: teacher at **Hindu College (1826–31)**, inspired **Young Bengal**
- Vidyasagar: **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**
- Vidyasagar: principal of **Sanskrit College**; **Barnaparichay**
:::

:::caution
Do not confuse the two Calcutta colleges: **Derozio** taught at the **Hindu College**; **Vidyasagar** headed the **Sanskrit College**.
:::

## Prarthana Samaj and Arya Samaj {#prarthana-arya}

### Prarthana Samaj (1867)
- Founded in **Bombay in 1867** by **Atmaram Pandurang**, inspired by **Keshab Chandra Sen's** visit.
- Its ideas were close to those of the Brahmo Samaj: worship of one God, and social reform.
- **M.G. Ranade** and **R.G. Bhandarkar** were its leading members.
- Its main social programme: **women's education, widow remarriage, raising the age of marriage and opposition to caste discrimination**.
- It drew strongly on the devotional (bhakti) tradition of the Maharashtrian saint-poets.

### Arya Samaj (1875)
- Founded in **Bombay in 1875** by **Swami Dayananda Saraswati (1824–1883)**, born in **Gujarat (Kathiawar)**. Its main centres later developed in **Punjab**.
- Dayananda regarded the **Vedas** as the source of true knowledge and called for a return to them, a message summed up as **"Back to the Vedas"**. He rejected the later Puranas, idol worship, ritualism and the authority of priests.
- His major book is **Satyarth Prakash**.
- Social programme: opposed **untouchability, caste by birth and child marriage**; supported **women's education and widow remarriage**.
- **Shuddhi** (purification): a movement to bring back into Hinduism those who had converted to other religions.
- **Education**: after Dayananda's death his followers founded **Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV)** schools and colleges, the first at **Lahore**, which combined modern Western education with Vedic studies. A section led by **Swami Shraddhanand** preferred a traditional pattern and founded the **Gurukul at Kangri, near Haridwar**.

:::keyfacts
- Prarthana Samaj: **1867, Bombay**, Atmaram Pandurang; **M.G. Ranade**
- Arya Samaj: **1875, Bombay**, **Dayananda Saraswati**
- Book: **Satyarth Prakash**; slogan: **"Back to the Vedas"**; **shuddhi**
:::

:::caution
Both the **Prarthana Samaj** and the **Arya Samaj** were founded in **Bombay**. The Arya Samaj became strongest in **Punjab**, not Maharashtra.
:::

## Ramakrishna Mission and Theosophical Society {#ramakrishna-theosophy}

### Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and Swami Vivekananda
- **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (1836–1886)** was a priest at the **Dakshineswar** temple near Calcutta. He taught that **all religions are different paths to the same God**, and stressed devotion and the service of human beings.
- His chief disciple, **Swami Vivekananda (Narendranath Datta, 1863–1902)**, spread these ideas in India and abroad.
- At the **Parliament of Religions in Chicago (1893)**, Vivekananda presented Vedanta and the idea of the harmony of religions, which won him wide attention in the West.
- He founded the **Ramakrishna Mission in 1897**. Its headquarters is at **Belur, near Calcutta**.
- The Mission combines spiritual teaching with **social service**: schools, hospitals, orphanages and relief work during famines and floods. Vivekananda saw service to the poor as service to God.
- He condemned caste rigidity and untouchability and urged Indians to develop self-confidence and physical and moral strength.

### Theosophical Society
- Founded in **New York in 1875** by **Madame H.P. Blavatsky** (a Russian) and **Colonel H.S. Olcott** (an American).
- Its headquarters later moved to **Adyar, near Madras**.
- It drew on Hinduism, Buddhism and the idea of universal brotherhood, and encouraged pride in ancient Indian religions and philosophy.
- **Annie Besant**, of Irish descent, came to India in **1893** and became its leading figure in India. She founded the **Central Hindu College at Benaras (1898)**, which later became part of the **Banaras Hindu University**.

:::keyfacts
- Chicago Parliament of Religions: **1893**
- Ramakrishna Mission: **1897**, headquarters **Belur**
- Theosophical Society: **1875, New York**; HQ **Adyar**; **Annie Besant**
:::

:::caution
The **Ramakrishna Mission** was founded by **Vivekananda (1897)**, not by Ramakrishna, who had died in 1886. The **Theosophical Society** was founded in **New York**, not in India.
:::

:::mnemonic
**1875 had three births**: **A**rya Samaj (Bombay), **T**heosophical Society (New York), **M**AO College (Aligarh) – "**A**ll **T**hree **M**atter".
:::

## Muslim reform movements {#muslim-reform}

Reform among Muslims began somewhat later. After 1857 many Muslims were wary of Western education, and reformers tried to change this.

### Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement
- **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–1898)** believed that Muslims must take up **modern Western education and science** to progress.
- He interpreted the **Quran** in the light of reason and argued that there was no conflict between Islam and modern science.
- He founded the **Scientific Society (1864)** to translate English works on science into Urdu.
- In **1875** he founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College at Aligarh**, which later became the **Aligarh Muslim University (1920)**. (It opened in 1875 as a school; college classes began in 1877. Exam books give 1875.)
- The movement centred on Aligarh is called the **Aligarh Movement**.

### Deoband Movement
- A revivalist movement begun at the **Dar-ul-Ulum, Deoband** (in present Uttar Pradesh) in **1866** by **Muhammad Qasim Nanautavi** and **Rashid Ahmad Gangohi**.
- It aimed to spread what it regarded as the pure teachings of the Quran and Hadith and to train religious leaders. Unlike Aligarh, it did not seek Western education.

:::keyfacts
- MAO College, Aligarh: **1875**, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
- Became Aligarh Muslim University: **1920**
- Deoband: **1866**, revivalist, traditional religious learning
:::

:::exam-tip
Contrast **Aligarh** (modern Western education, reformist) with **Deoband** (traditional religious education, revivalist). Questions often test this difference.
:::

## Anti-caste movements {#caste-movements}

Some of the strongest reform movements came from groups that suffered under the caste system. Their leaders challenged Brahmanical dominance and demanded education and dignity.

### Jyotirao (Jyotiba) Phule
- **Jyotirao Phule (1827–1890)** of Maharashtra came from the Mali (gardener) community.
- With his wife **Savitribai Phule**, he opened a **school for girls in Poona (1848)** and later schools for lower-caste children.
- He founded the **Satyashodhak Samaj** ("Society of Truth Seekers") in **1873** to work for the rights of lower castes and women and against Brahmanical dominance.
- His book **Gulamgiri** ("Slavery", 1873) compared the condition of lower castes with slavery.
- He also worked for widow remarriage.

### Sree Narayana Guru
- **Sree Narayana Guru (1856–1928)** was a religious and social reformer of **Kerala** from the **Ezhava** community, which suffered from caste discrimination.
- He founded the **Sree Narayana Dharma Paripalana (SNDP) Yogam in 1903** to work for the social and educational progress of the Ezhavas.
- He set up temples open to all and spread his well-known message, **"One caste, one religion, one God for mankind"**.

:::keyfacts
- Satyashodhak Samaj: **1873**, **Jyotiba Phule**; book **Gulamgiri**
- Girls' school at Poona: **1848**, Jyotiba and **Savitribai Phule**
- SNDP Yogam: **1903**, **Sree Narayana Guru**, Kerala (Ezhavas)
:::

## Key social laws and summary {#social-laws}

The reformers' campaigns led the colonial government to pass several laws. Laws alone did not change social practice quickly, but they gave legal backing to reform.

| Law | Year | Main provision | Linked with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulation XVII | 1829 | Sati declared illegal | Rammohun Roy; Lord William Bentinck |
| Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act | 1856 | Remarriage of Hindu widows made legal | Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar |
| Native Marriage Act (Brahmo Marriage Act) | 1872 | Legalised Brahmo and inter-caste marriages; minimum marriage age | Keshab Chandra Sen |
| Age of Consent Act | 1891 | Raised the age of consent for girls from 10 to 12 years | Campaign by B.M. Malabari |
| Child Marriage Restraint Act (Sarda Act) | 1929 | Fixed minimum marriage age (14 for girls, 18 for boys) | Harbilas Sarda |

| Atmiya Sabha | Rammohun Roy | 1814 (or 1815) | Calcutta |
| Brahmo Sabha | Rammohun Roy | 1828 | Calcutta |
| Tattvabodhini Sabha | Debendranath Tagore | 1839 | Calcutta |
| Prarthana Samaj | Atmaram Pandurang | 1867 | Bombay |
| Satyashodhak Samaj | Jyotiba Phule | 1873 | Maharashtra |
| Arya Samaj | Dayananda Saraswati | 1875 | Bombay |

### Significance of the reform movements
- They spread **rational and humanitarian thinking** and weakened superstition.
- They improved the position of **women** through education and legal reform.
- They challenged **caste discrimination** and untouchability.
- They built **pride and self-confidence** in Indian culture, which later fed into national consciousness.
- Limitations: most movements reached mainly the **urban educated classes**, and some later took on a narrow religious tone.
